Armpit pain without other symptoms is often caused by minor issues such as a mild skin infection, muscle strain, irritation from shaving or deodorant, or a swollen lymph node. In most cases, it resolves on its own. However, unexplained or persistent armpit pain can occasionally point to underlying conditions—like an infection, a cyst, nerve irritation, or in rare cases, something more serious—that may benefit from medical evaluation.

Explanation

Sometimes, people feel pain in their armpit even when nothing else seems wrong. One possibility is that the lymph nodes in your armpit, which help fight off germs, can get a little swollen or tender. This is called adenopathy. A small infection or even mild irritation can make these nodes feel sore, even if you don't feel sick elsewhere.

In rare instances, the pain could be related to problems with the nerves in that area or early signs of something like a tumor. However, if you only have armpit pain and no other symptoms such as fever, unusual lumps, or skin changes, it is usually not a sign of a serious illness. Many issues that cause isolated pain are temporary and harmless.

Still, if the pain continues for a long time, over 2 weeks, becomes worse, or if you develop other changes, it's important to talk to a doctor.
