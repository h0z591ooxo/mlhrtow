Explanation

Swelling in the right arm may result from a variety of causes, including:

Injury or trauma: A muscle strain, sprain, or even repetitive motion can lead to swelling. Infection: Skin infections like cellulitis can cause swelling, especially if the area is also red or warm. Blood clot (deep vein thrombosis): A clot can block blood flow in a vein, leading to sudden swelling. Lymphedema: A buildup of lymph fluid from blocked or damaged lymph vessels can cause persistent swelling. Allergic reaction: Exposure to allergens (e.g., insect bites or stings) may lead to swelling in one arm. Venous or lymphatic obstruction: Blockages in blood or lymph vessels can cause localized fluid retention. Systemic issues: Conditions like heart, liver, or kidney disease may lead to swelling, though these usually affect more than one limb.

If the swelling is sudden, painful, or accompanied by symptoms like redness, heat, numbness, or shortness of breath, it’s important to seek medical care.
