Is It Liver Pain? Why Your Liver Hurts & Medically Approved Next Steps

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Upper right abdominal pain often points to the liver or nearby organs. The liver itself typically hurts only when swelling stretches its outer capsule. Common causes include fatty liver disease, hepatitis, alcohol-related liver disease, liver abscess, cirrhosis, or liver cancer. Gallstones, bile duct issues, and other conditions can also mimic liver pain, making an accurate assessment essential.

Seek urgent care immediately for red flags: severe pain, jaundice (yellowing skin or eyes), fever with chills, vomiting blood, black stools, confusion, or sudden abdominal swelling. Otherwise, schedule a medical evaluation for liver blood tests and imaging, review your alcohol intake and medications, and start liver-friendly lifestyle changes.

Explanation

If you're feeling discomfort in the upper right side of your abdomen, you may be wondering: Is this liver pain?

It's a common concern. The liver is a vital organ, and when something doesn't feel right in that area, it's natural to worry. The good news is that not all upper abdominal pain is serious — but some causes do require medical attention.

Here's what you need to know about liver pain, why it happens, and what to do next.

Where Is Liver Pain Felt?

The liver sits in the upper right side of your abdomen, just under your rib cage. It's protected by your lower ribs.

When people describe liver pain, they often say it feels like:

A dull ache in the upper right abdomen

A feeling of fullness or pressure

Tenderness when pressing the area

Pain that may radiate to the right shoulder or back

However, it's important to know something surprising:

The liver itself doesn't have many pain-sensitive nerves.Pain usually happens when the liver swells or stretches its outer covering (the capsule).

Because of this, what feels like liver pain can sometimes come from nearby organs such as the gallbladder, stomach, pancreas, or muscles.

Common Causes of Liver Pain

Several medical conditions can cause true liver pain. Some are mild and reversible. Others require prompt medical care.

1. Fatty Liver Disease

This is one of the most common liver conditions. It occurs when fat builds up inside liver cells.

There are two main types:

Nonalcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD) – Often linked to obesity, diabetes, or high cholesterol

Alcohol-related fatty liver disease – Caused by heavy alcohol use

Fatty liver disease often causes no symptoms, but some people experience:

Mild upper right abdominal discomfort

Fatigue

General feeling of fullness

The good news: Early fatty liver disease can often be improved with lifestyle changes.

2. Hepatitis (Liver Inflammation)

Hepatitis means inflammation of the liver. It may be caused by:

Viral infections (Hepatitis A, B, or C)

Alcohol

Certain medications

Autoimmune diseases

Symptoms may include:

Liver pain or tenderness

Nausea

Yellowing of the skin or eyes (jaundice)

Dark urine

Some forms of hepatitis resolve on their own, while others require long-term treatment.

3. Alcohol-Related Liver Disease

Heavy drinking over time can lead to:

Fatty liver

Alcoholic hepatitis

Cirrhosis (scarring of the liver)

Pain may occur as the liver becomes inflamed or enlarged. This is a serious condition that requires medical care.

4. Liver Abscess

A liver abscess is a pocket of infection in the liver. It's less common but can be serious.

Sharp or severe liver pain

Fever

Chills

Sweating

This condition requires urgent medical treatment.

5. Cirrhosis

Cirrhosis is advanced scarring of the liver caused by long-term damage. It may develop from:

Chronic hepatitis

Alcohol use

Long-standing fatty liver disease

Early cirrhosis may not cause noticeable pain. As it progresses, symptoms can include:

Abdominal swelling

Easy bruising

Confusion

Cirrhosis is serious and needs close medical management.

6. Liver Cancer

Liver cancer is less common than other causes of abdominal pain, but it can cause:

Persistent liver pain

Unexplained weight loss

Loss of appetite

Swelling in the abdomen

Early evaluation is critical if symptoms persist.

Conditions That Feel Like Liver Pain (But Aren't)

Not all right-sided abdominal pain comes from the liver. Other common causes include:

Gallstones – Often cause sharp pain after eating fatty meals

Gallbladder inflammation

Muscle strain

Acid reflux

Kidney issues

Pancreatitis

Because many conditions overlap in symptoms, it can be challenging to determine what's causing your discomfort. When Is Liver Pain an Emergency?

Some symptoms require immediate medical attention.

Seek urgent care if you have:

Severe or worsening abdominal pain

Yellowing of the eyes or skin

High fever with chills

Vomiting blood

Black or tarry stools

Confusion or extreme drowsiness

Sudden swelling of the abdomen

These can signal serious liver problems or other life-threatening conditions.

Do not delay seeking emergency care if these occur.

How Doctors Diagnose Liver Pain

If you see a healthcare provider for suspected liver pain, they may:

Ask about alcohol use, medications, and medical history

Perform a physical exam

Order blood tests (liver function tests)

Recommend imaging (ultrasound, CT scan, or MRI)

In some cases, suggest a liver biopsy

Blood tests are often the first step and can detect inflammation or liver damage even before symptoms are severe.

Medically Approved Next Steps

If you suspect liver pain, here's a practical, evidence-based approach:

1. Don't Ignore Persistent Pain

If pain lasts more than a few days, worsens, or keeps returning, schedule a doctor's appointment.

Early evaluation can prevent complications.

2. Review Alcohol Intake

If you drink alcohol:

Consider reducing or stopping

Avoid binge drinking

Discuss safe limits with your doctor

The liver can heal from early damage if stressors are removed.

3. Review Medications

Some medications and supplements can strain the liver.

Never stop prescribed medication on your own — but do ask your doctor whether anything you're taking could affect your liver.

4. Support Liver Health

Evidence supports the following lifestyle steps:

Maintain a healthy weight

Exercise regularly (at least 150 minutes per week)

Eat a balanced diet rich in vegetables, whole grains, and lean protein

Limit processed foods and added sugars

Control blood sugar and cholesterol

5. Get Proper Testing

If liver disease is suspected, blood work and imaging are essential. Many liver conditions are manageable when caught early.

Can Liver Pain Go Away on Its Own?

Sometimes, yes — especially if it's related to:

Mild inflammation

Temporary infection

Minor alcohol-related irritation

But ongoing or worsening liver pain should never be ignored. Chronic liver disease can progress silently for years.

That's why timely evaluation matters.

Liver pain is real — but it's not always the liver.
