# Drinking Alcohol on Insulin? Read This Critical Warning Now | Ubie Doctor's Note

Published on: 7/21/2026

Can I drink alcohol on insulin?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

[Drinking Alcohol on](https://ageless-peptides.china-akan.com/)

Internal Medicine

Yes, you can drink alcohol while taking insulin, but it requires caution. Alcohol can cause unpredictable changes in blood sugar—often leading to hypoglycemia (low blood sugar) hours after drinking—and it may also affect how your body responds to insulin.

Key considerations include:

Never drink on an empty stomach, as this raises the risk of dangerous lows.

Monitor your blood sugar before, during, and after drinking.

Limit intake (generally no more than 1 drink/day for women, 2 for men).

Avoid sugary mixers that can spike blood sugar.

Because alcohol's effects on insulin and blood sugar vary from person to person, symptoms like dizziness, confusion, or fatigue can be easily mistaken for intoxication—delaying critical care. Explanation

Drinking alcohol can change how your body handles insulin and blood sugar. Studies show that alcohol can make your body less sensitive to insulin, meaning it might not work as well. This can lead to higher blood sugar levels. However, in some cases, alcohol can also cause blood sugar to drop too low, especially if you drink on an empty stomach or take insulin.

Key Points to Consider:

Monitor Blood Sugar: If you drink alcohol, keep an eye on your blood sugar levels. Alcohol can cause both high and low blood sugar.

Eat Before Drinking: Eating food can help keep your blood sugar stable when drinking.
