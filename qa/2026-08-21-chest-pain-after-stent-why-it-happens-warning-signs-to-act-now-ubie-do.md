Why might I still experience chest pain after having a stent placed?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Chest pain after a stent placement can occur for several reasons, even when the procedure was successful. Common causes include:

[Signs to Act](https://focus-and-calm.sunsimiaosd.com/)

Narrowing in other coronary arteries that weren't stented

Restenosis (re-narrowing of the stented artery over time)

In-stent thrombosis (a clot forming within the stent)

Non-cardiac causes such as acid reflux, muscle strain, or anxiety

Microvascular disease affecting smaller heart vessels

Persistent or new chest pain after stenting should always be evaluated by a doctor promptly, as it may signal a serious issue requiring intervention.

A stent is placed to open a blocked part of a heart artery, but it doesn't fix all the problems that might be causing chest pain. One reason is that you could have narrowing in other nearby arteries that were not treated during the stenting. Sometimes, the artery where the stent was placed may become narrow again, a condition called in-stent restenosis. In other cases, the heart muscle might still be under stress because of factors such as spasms in the artery or other ongoing heart issues. It's also possible that the chest pain could be coming from the muscles or nerves around the heart that were affected during or after the procedure. These possibilities mean that even after the stent is in place, chest pain may still occur. It is important to share these symptoms with your doctor so that they can check for any changes and run further tests.
