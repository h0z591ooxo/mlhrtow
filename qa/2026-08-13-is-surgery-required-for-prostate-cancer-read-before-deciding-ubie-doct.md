Is surgery always required for prostate cancer?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Prostate cancer does not always require surgery. Treatment depends on factors like cancer stage, grade, age, overall health, and personal preference. Options may include active surveillance (close monitoring), radiation therapy, hormone therapy, chemotherapy, or focal therapies. For slow-growing or early-stage cancers, monitoring is often preferred to avoid side effects of surgery.

Treatment Options:

Active Surveillance: For some men, especially those with low-risk cancer, doctors might suggest just watching the cancer carefully without immediate treatment.

Radiation Therapy: This can be an option for some, using high-energy rays to kill cancer cells.

Surgery: Some may choose surgery to remove the prostate, especially if the cancer is more aggressive.

Making the Decision:

The decision-making process is personal and involves weighing the benefits and risks of each treatment option.

Patients often consider how each treatment might affect their quality of life and what feels right for them.
