Brain fog is a feeling of being slow or a little confused, and it happens for many different reasons. Researchers have found that brain fog can be related to stress, lack of sleep, poor diet, or other health issues. Because of the many reasons it happens, there isn’t a magic trick or medicine that can clear it up immediately. For example, one study showed that a special treatment called neurofeedback helped some people with COVID-19-related brain fog, but it does not work instantly—it takes time to see improvements. Other research has explored what brain fog is and how it affects people, but these studies indicate that treating brain fog usually means taking care of your overall health. This might involve getting better sleep, managing stress, eating healthy foods, and exercising. Meditation has also been shown to improve memory and cognition. If you are dealing with brain fog, it is a good idea to talk with a doctor or health professional who can help make a plan that is right for your needs. Remember, improvements often come slowly, not all at once.
