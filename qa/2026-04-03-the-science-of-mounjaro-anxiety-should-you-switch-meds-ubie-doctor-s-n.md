What Is Mounjaro and How Does It Work?

Mounjaro belongs to a class called GIP/GLP-1 receptor agonists. It mimics the hormones GIP (glucose-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide) and GLP-1 (glucagon-like peptide-1), which:

Stimulate insulin release when blood sugar is high

Slow down stomach emptying

Reduce appetite

By acting on receptors in the pancreas and brain, Mounjaro helps control blood sugar and promotes weight loss.

Several factors may explain why some people experience anxiety or anxiety-like symptoms when taking Mounjaro:

Gastrointestinal Side Effects

Nausea, bloating, diarrhea or constipation are common in the first weeks.

Physical discomfort can trigger stress and nervousness.

Blood Sugar Fluctuations

Rapid drops in blood sugar (hypoglycemia) can cause shakiness, racing heart, sweating and feeling "on edge."

Even mild low-blood-sugar episodes can mimic anxiety.

Central Nervous System Effects

GLP-1 receptors are present in areas of the brain that regulate mood and stress.

Altering these signals might contribute to feelings of restlessness or jitteriness.

Hormonal and Metabolic Changes

Weight loss and changes in metabolism can affect cortisol and adrenaline levels.

Elevated stress hormones may lead to a heightened "fight or flight" response.

Psychological Factors

Starting a new medication can cause worry about possible side effects.

Monitoring diet, injections and health markers adds mental load.
