# How Severe Can Sciatica Pain Get? Doctor’s Warning: Act Now | Ubie Doctor's Note

How severe can sciatica pain get?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Sciatica pain can range from mild to severe, often causing sharp, shooting, or burning pain that radiates from the lower back down through the buttock and leg. In severe cases, it can make it difficult to walk, stand, or even sit comfortably, and may be accompanied by numbness, tingling, or muscle weakness.

Because sciatica symptoms can overlap with other conditions—such as a herniated disc, piriformis syndrome, or spinal stenosis—understanding the underlying cause is key to getting the right care. Explanation

Sciatica pain can vary from mild to very severe. It often starts in the lower back and travels down the leg. This pain can feel like a sharp, shooting sensation that makes it difficult to move or stand. Some people may also experience a burning feeling or numbness in the leg.

Severity and Impact

Mild Pain: Some people feel a dull ache or mild discomfort. It might not stop them from doing daily activities.

Severe Pain: For others, the pain can be intense and sharp. It may make walking, sitting, or even standing difficult.

Causes and Challenges

The pain comes from irritation or pressure on the sciatic nerve. This can happen because of a slipped disc or other spine problems. Treating sciatica can be challenging, especially in older adults, because they might have other health issues.
