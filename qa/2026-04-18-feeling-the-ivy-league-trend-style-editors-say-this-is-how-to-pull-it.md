# Feeling the Ivy League Trend? Style Editors Say This Is How to Pull It Off.

Our product picks are editor-tested, expert-approved. We may earn a commission through links on our site. Why Trust Us?

IVY LEAGUE STYLE is having a full-circle moment right now. Twenty years ago, it had a reputation for being buttoned-up and reserved for prep school, East Coast guys. At its core, Ivy style is made up of bright knits, clean-cut trousers, and oxford shirts. But thanks to forward thinking designers like Aimé Leon Dore, Barbour, and Rowing Blazers who have remixed those preppy looks with more relaxed fits, richer textures, and everyday colors that make it feel a lot less stiff.

[Style Editors Say](https://defensecascade.china-akan.com/)

Now, guys on my FYP page are pairing vintage varsity pieces with sleek fits, styling socks with loafers, and leaning into that preppy vibe. And even non-fashion dudes have caught wind of the trend. Office guys are in pleated chinos, loafers, and crisp oxfords. It looks straight out of a modern prep playbook, but much more accessible. In fact, mall brands like J. Crew, Bonobos, and L.L. Bean make staples that tap into the aesthetic without breaking the bank.

As for myself, I’ve been hooked on this look since my days at Howard University, where students prioritized dressing sharp. “Looking good” was a statement and you had to come correct. The yard was basically a runway, and you learned fast that a clean oxford, polished loafers, and a proper fit could say as much as your GPA. I’d go to class in cable-knit sweaters and chinos, maybe throw on a tweed blazer if I wanted to channel the journalist I was studying to become. That early love for Ivy style never really left me; it’s shaped my personal style—polished but relaxed, timeless with just enough personality to feel fresh.

Even pop culture has jumped in. Stylish guys like Jacob Elordi, Timothée Chalamet, and Tyler, the Creator have been snapped in Ivy-coded fits that feel like a nod to the classics while still feeling current. But here’s the thing: you don’t need to be an East Coast elitist to pull this off. Start small—an oxford shirt, a crewneck sweater, a simple pair of loafers. If you don’t know where to start, I’ve rounded up a few key pieces that tap into the Ivy League trend without screaming it.

Crewneck Sweaters and Knits

If there’s one piece that defines Ivy style, it’s the classic crewneck. I’ve always loved how easy it is to throw on—over an oxford, under a blazer, or just on its own. Back in college, a cable-knit or a crest-emblazoned sweater meant you were taking your look seriously, even if you were just heading to class. Today, brands like Aimé Leon Dore and Abercrombie are giving that same energy new life, with chunkier textures and looser fits that make them feel more laid-back but still sharp.

Rugby Shirts

A rugby shirt is the perfect midpoint between a polo and a sweatshirt—structured, sporty, and just thick enough to feel substantial without being heavy. Back in college, I wore mine the classic way: bold stripes, stiff collar, and usually layered over an oxford when I wanted to look like I had my life together. These days, I treat it more like a smart casual staple. I’ll throw one on with relaxed chinos or jeans, sometimes even under a blazer for that mix of prep and ease that feels very right-now. Labels like J. Crew and Rowing Blazers nail this look better than anybody.

Blazers and Sport Coats

The blazer is where Ivy style shows its polish. It’s the piece that instantly elevates anything—jeans, chinos, even sweats if you’re feeling bold. I used to reach for a tweed sport coat when I wanted to channel that “future news anchor” energy, and I still keep one in rotation. Today’s versions are cut a little roomier now, like Ralph Lauren’s latest Polo jackets, which feel broken-in right out of the gate.

Chinos and Trousers

[League Trend Style](https://focus-and-calm.sunsimiaosd.com/)

No Ivy-inspired look works without a good pair of chinos. I’ve worn mine every which way—pressed and neat for the office, cuffed and casual on weekends. They’re the backbone of the style: versatile, unfussy, and timeless. The current wave leans looser and pleated, which gives them a relaxed vibe that’s still smart. From workout brands such as lululemon to menswear giants like Bonobos, they make excellent options with stretchy fabrics and technical features like moisture-wicking and quick-drying properties.

Loafers

Then there are the loafers. Clean, classic, and quietly confident. I wore mine with white socks in college and still do—it’s a little rebellious, a little old-school, and somehow always works. Brands like G.H. Bass, Morjas, and Suitsupply make the gold-standard versions.

Ivy League style has stuck around this long because it just works. The clothes are simple, the fits are sharp, and everything plays well together. Whether it’s an oxford shirt with a sweater tied around your neck or loafers styled with socks, every piece has purpose. That’s why, decades later, it still feels right for how guys want to dress today.
