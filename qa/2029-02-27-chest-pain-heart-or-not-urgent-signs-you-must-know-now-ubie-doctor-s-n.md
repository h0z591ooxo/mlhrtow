Chest pain can have many causes, and understanding the type of pain is key. Heart-related chest pain typically feels like pressure, tightness, or squeezing in the center of the chest and is often accompanied by shortness of breath, sweating, nausea, or pain radiating to the arm, jaw, or back. In contrast, non-cardiac chest pain—such as from muscle strain, acid reflux, or anxiety—tends to feel sharp, stabbing, or localized, and often worsens with movement, breathing, or pressing on the area.

Because chest pain can signal anything from a minor issue to a medical emergency, identifying the cause early matters. Explanation

Chest pain can be scary and it is important to know where it might be coming from. Heart-related chest pain is often described as a pressure or squeezing feeling in the chest. It might also come with other symptoms like shortness of breath, sweating, or feeling sick to your stomach.

Heart vs. Other Causes:

Heart-related pain: This type of pain can feel like a heavy weight pressing on your chest. It might spread to your arms, neck, or back, and usually happens during activity or stress.

Other causes: Pain from muscles or bones usually feels different. It might be sharp or get worse when you move or touch your chest. Sometimes, athletes have pain from muscles or bones because they use their chest a lot.

If you ever have chest pain, you can start by using a free symptom checker to help understand what might be causing your discomfort, but it is important to talk to a doctor to get the right care and find out if it is from your heart or something else.
