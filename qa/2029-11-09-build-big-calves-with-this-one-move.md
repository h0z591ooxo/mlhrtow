# Build Big Calves With This One Move

WHEN YOU THINK about leg day, you likely think about training your glutes, hamstrings, and quads. There's one muscle group that's often forgotten about, and that's the calf muscles. To create a well-rounded physique, you'll want to start incorporating this vital muscle into your leg day routine. One of the best exercises to do that is the standing calf raise.

"The great thing about training our calves is it's not incredibly complicated," says Men's Health fitness director Ebenezer Samuel, C.S.C.S.. You don't need to incorporate a bunch of exercises to hit this important muscle group—the standing calf raise alone will help you build the muscle you need.

You don't need a machine readily available to do it, either. Here, Samuel lays out the intricacies of the standing calf raise, as well as how to do it with just a barbell and a rack.

Aesthetics

You've probably made fun of your friends with skinny calves before. It's an ongoing joke among gym-goers. Not training your calves can make your leg gains look a bit lopsided. "Our calves really bring our physique into balance and give us that nice, complete look," Samuel says.

Muscle Building

If you're trying to build strong legs, you need to be working out your calves just as much as you do all the other major muscle groups in your legs—and not only for aesthetic purposes. Building muscle in our calves helps the functionality of movements like walking and running, promotes joint stability, and will help better your balance.

The standing calf raise targets the biggest and outer most portion of our calf muscles, the gastrocnemius. We do this by training ankle extension, which is the "fundamental motion" of our calf muscles, Samuel says, which is the act of lifting our heels up off the ground, and driving our weight up onto our toes.

How to Do the Standing Calf Raise Without a Machine

Grab a small step to elevate your heels. Situate it a little bit behind the rack—about four to five inches back.

Stabilize the step by placing a heavy dumbbell or kettlebell in front of it. This will prevent it from sliding.

Situate the safety bars so they are a little lower than your shoulders when you're standing up on the step.

For best results, take off your shoes before you begin the movement. Some stiffer shoes, like high tops, won't allow for proper range of motion you need to properly execute this move.

Bring your body underneath the bar. Think about pulling your elbows down and back in line with your torso to tighten up the upper body. The bar should situate right on your upper back—not your neck.

From here, lean the bar into the rack. This eliminates the challenge of balance, allowing you to really load up the weight once you're ready for it.

Slowly lower your heels down to the ground to where you feel a good stretch through your calves. Hold that stretch for 2 seconds or so before pushing through your toes to power upward.

As you drive up, think about continuously driving the bar into the rack. Squeeze at the top, when your ankles are fully extended.

How to Add the Standing Calf Raise to Your Workouts

Every time you take a step, your calves put in a little bit of work. So, they're meant to take a lot of reps. Because of that, Samuel suggests aiming for 20 to 25 reps for 3 to 4 sets.
