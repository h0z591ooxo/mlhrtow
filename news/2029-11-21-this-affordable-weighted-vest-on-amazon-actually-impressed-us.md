WALKING HAS BECOME a daily ritual for me. Aviators? Check. Walking shoes? Check. My 11-month-old? Double check. That’s been my pre-walk checklist for the last six months since I entered full-time stay-at-home-dad mode. Recently, though, I’ve added one more item to that list: a weighted vest. Not the kind reserved for Navy SEAL-inspired workouts, but the APEXUP. It’s a compact, neoprene and iron sand shell you’ve probably scrolled past on Instagram.

Weighted vests have been blowing up online, with posts of people—not unattainably jacked fitness influencers, either, but middle-aged men and women—who want to burn more calories, increase bone strength, and further engage their core during their walks. They’re turning to vests to improve overall health. And while weighted walks are the main trend blowing up, vests are an often-forgotten tool for loading all types of exercises.

“I think we tend to be very one-dimensional when we think about load in training, that it has to be dumbbells or barbells or kettlebells, but a weighted vest is an easy way that we can add a load to things without our grip being the mitigating factor,” explains Men’s Health Fitness Director Ebenezer Samuel, C.S,C.S. “That said, it's a little bit ridiculous when people try to claim that it's some miracle tool for improving your metabolism. It’s just another way to add load, so to the extent that any load increase can slightly increase burn more calories, so can a weighted vest.” Recent studies support this point by Samuel.

Men’s Health has tested dozens of vests and crowned its favorites, but even the best ones will slim out your wallet in addition to your waistline (they are quality picks, though). To appeal to the budget-minded reader, MH asked APEXUP to send me one of its vests for review. It’s cheaper, easy to buy on Amazon, and—judging by my feed—one of the more popular options out there.

How I Tested This Vest

For two weeks, I strapped on the APEXUP vest for my weekday routine: two daily walks ranging from 30 to 45 minutes. That’s 10 walks a week, every week, which I think is enough time to test how it holds up to consistent use. I also cycled through a circuit of classic bodyweight moves—pushups, pull-ups, reverse lunges, squats, and yes, even burpees—to see how the vest felt when I moved more explosively.

Fitness and Commerce Editor Charles Thorp, NASM, has also been training with the APEXUP for months and was excited for me to try it. His routine included going out to his local outdoor calisthenics gym, to use the weighted vest for pull-ups, dips, incline pushups, squats, and dead hangs. Also a new dad, he keeps one in the bottom pocket of his jogging stroller so that he can throw it on during power walks with his daughter.

A quick note: I consulted with Samuel after my testing period, and he suggests that most people shouldn’t wear weighted vests for athletic exercises like skater hops, box jumps, broad jumps, unless under the supervision of a professional. Even a few additional pounds can drastically impact your form and increase the pressure on your joints while landing during high-impact movements. “You’re better off doing those movements with your body weight unless you have a very specific program,” says Samuel.”

The APEXUP is a fixed-weight vest, so you need to pick your weight before buying one. A good rule of thumb is to start with five to 10 percent of your bodyweight if you’re new, and 10 to 15 percent if you’ve been training for a while. Explosive moves—box jumps, broad jumps, burpees—require a lighter load. APEXUP also provides guidelines based on your weight. I landed on the 20-pound option, about 11 percent of my bodyweight, since I planned on mostly walking while wearing it.

The vest itself is built from neoprene, a durable synthetic rubber, and filled with iron sand. Iron sand—which is made up mostly of the mineral magnetite—is denser than natural sand and absorbs less moisture, so the vest doesn’t feel bloated or soggy after a sweat session.

Some companies warn that iron sand can leak through porous neoprene, which could cause skin irritation or allergic reactions. I didn’t run into either issue, but if you have sensitive skin or are picky about materials, it’s something to keep in mind.

The Fit: Comfortable Yet Bulky

I wore the APEXUP vest for about 20 hours over two weeks. If I ever wanted to take it off, it wasn’t because the vest was uncomfortable. Whether I wore it over a t-shirt or directly on my skin, the neoprene was smooth and non-abrasive. The seams are stitched over with fabric, so nothing rubbed or chafed, even on longer walks or when the vest shifted around during dynamic exercises.

Before researching for this review, I would’ve thought “iron sand” was a place out of a J.R.R. Tolkien novel, and not a sand substitute. Regardless of the skepticism around it, this wannabe sand performed well. It was dense enough that it didn’t shift or clump with enough give to keep my collarbones and shoulders from taking a beating.

I’ll tell you what this vest ain’t: stylish. Like a souped-up Honda Civic, it’s got plenty of function, and little else going for it. You can buy the vest in 17 in different colors, all of which are bright, gaudy even. Its silhouette is somewhat backpack-like, as though someone removed the “pack” and tightened the two straps as tightly as possible. It’s not discreet at all, and it rides up high, looping down over your shoulders and chest, extending to halfway down your torso. It's compact compared to other vests that envelop your entire torso, but, as a result, it's bulkier and less sleek.

Still, even without the cool factor, I feel like John Wick when I strap into the APEXUP. Except, instead of fighting off assassins, I’m defending my aging bones from the terror of osteoporosis.

Training in the APEXUP Weighted Vest

Thanks to the wide arm holes and looped design, the vest is simple to put on. So simple that I slipped into it while holding my son—sliding one arm in, transferring my son to my other side, and slipping the other arm in. (You haven’t seen John Wick do that, have you?)

The vest would annoyingly shift positions if I didn’t pull the straps tight. I’m not a narrow-shouldered person. Still, the straps felt like they could slip off to the side. They never did, but if you’re a smaller-framed person, make the vest tight. The straps are excessively long, way longer than they need to be, but I’d just tuck them inside the vest. Problem solved.

The weight in the vest is a bit front-loaded so I felt it pulling my torso forward—you should maintain an upright posture while wearing a weighted vest, and just in general—but it never disrupted my workouts. A lot of the iron sand seems packed into the torso straps, and less so in the x-shaped back support that holds the vest together.

All in all, it’s a fine vest for walking. If you adjust it to fit snuggly, the APEXUP vest will stay put and sit comfortably on your shoulders. However, once you start moving with a purpose, things change.

The vest stayed in place during lunges, squats, and push-ups, but shifted during burpees. If you plan on performing a lot of dynamic exercises, opt for a vest that wraps around your entire torso for a more secure fit.

Value

The APEXUP Weighted Vest is a quality product for a very fair price, but that price comes with some trade-offs that you need to consider. Value means different things to different people. If you plan on hopping on the weighted vest walking trend, you absolutely don’t need a premium weighted vest.

However, if you plan on using it to add resistance to exercises, then you’ll probably be better off with an adjustable weight vest like a plate carrier. Or if you want a vest to wear while doing plyometric exercises or even movements like split squats, a vest like the Hyperwear Hyper Vest Elite, which is snug, light, and disperses weight over a larger surface area, is a better pick for you.
