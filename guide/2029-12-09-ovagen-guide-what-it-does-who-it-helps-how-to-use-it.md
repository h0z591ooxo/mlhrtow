What Is Ovagen?

Ovagen is a peptide bioregulator that targets the liver and the gastrointestinal tract. It belongs to the group of ultrashort regulatory peptides. Its sequence is glutamic acid, aspartic acid, and leucine. Researchers designed it to help normalize cellular functions in these organs. The aim is to support repair at the level of gene expression and improve liver resilience under stress.

The origin of Ovagen traces to Russian peptide research. The St. Petersburg Institute of Bioregulation and Gerontology led much of this work over several decades. Their teams studied how short peptides guide aging and organ repair. Ovagen grew from this program and appears in experimental and adjunct medical use for liver support. You will often see it called a “Cytogen,” which means a synthesized peptide rather than a tissue extract.

How Ovagen differs from other peptides:

It focuses on liver and gut tissue, while many peptides act more broadly.

Its short sequence uses peptide transporters for absorption, supporting better bioavailability.

The design aims to normalize function instead of forcing strong stimulant effects.

In stacks, it complements organ specific bioregulators. For example, Epitalon works on general aging pathways, while Ovagen centers on hepatobiliary repair.
