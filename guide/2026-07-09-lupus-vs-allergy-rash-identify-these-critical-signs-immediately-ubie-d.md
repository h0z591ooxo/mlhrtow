How do I know if my skin rash is from lupus or just an allergy?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Lupus rashes and allergy rashes differ in several key ways. A lupus rash often appears as a distinctive butterfly-shaped (malar) rash across the cheeks and nose, is typically triggered by sun exposure, and may feel warm rather than itchy. Allergy rashes, on the other hand, are usually itchy, can appear anywhere on the body, and often show up as hives, red bumps, or patches of irritated skin shortly after contact with an allergen.

Because these two conditions require very different treatments, identifying the cause of your rash quickly matters. Take a free, instant, online symptom check to better understand what may be behind your symptoms and confidently plan your next steps.

Explanation

When trying to figure out if a rash is from lupus or an allergy, it helps to look at how the rash looks and feels. Lupus rashes often appear as a butterfly shape across the cheeks and nose. They can be red and may not itch. Sometimes, lupus can cause other skin changes too.

Allergy rashes, on the other hand, can be different. They might be itchy and can appear anywhere on the body. They can look like hives or red patches.
