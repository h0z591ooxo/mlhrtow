What Is NMN?

NMN is a natural compound found in small amounts in fruits, vegetables and breast milk. It's a direct precursor to NAD⁺ (nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide), a molecule essential for:

Cellular energy production

DNA repair

Mitochondrial function

As we age, NAD⁺ levels decline. This drop is linked to slower metabolism, reduced cellular repair and—potentially—poorer egg quality.

Fertility After 40: The Biological Challenge

Women's fertility naturally decreases with age. After 35, and especially after 40, several changes occur:

Egg reserve declines

Mitochondrial function in eggs weakens

DNA damage accumulates

These factors can lead to:

Lower chances of conception per cycle

Higher risk of miscarriage

Greater likelihood of chromosomal abnormalities

While no supplement can completely reverse the clock, targeting mitochondrial health and cellular repair mechanisms is a logical strategy.

Why Your Doctor Might Suggest NMN

Emerging research (mostly in animals and early human studies) points to NMN's ability to:

Restoring NAD⁺ may enhance energy production in eggs.

Healthier mitochondria support better egg quality and division.

Optimal DNA repair may reduce the risk of aneuploidy (abnormal chromosome numbers).

In preclinical mouse models, NMN supplementation improved ovulation rates and offspring health. Small human trials (e.g., in women with diminished ovarian reserve) are underway to verify these findings.

Advanced maternal age (35+ or 40+)

Poor ovarian reserve (low AMH or high FSH levels)

Repeated IVF failure

Signs of mitochondrial dysfunction
