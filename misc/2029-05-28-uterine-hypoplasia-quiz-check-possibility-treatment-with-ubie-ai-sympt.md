What is Uterine Hypoplasia?

Uterine hypoplasia, also known as a hypoplastic uterus, occurs when a girl is born with an abnormally small uterus. Symptoms may include failure to start having periods at puberty (primary amenorrhea), abdominal pain, a small or no vaginal opening, or infertility.

Diagnostic Questions for Uterine Hypoplasia

Your doctor may ask these questions to check for this disease:

Is your menstrual cycle infrequent?

Do you hardly have any bleeding during your periods?

Have you been trying to become pregnant for more than a year without success?

Are your periods irregular?

Did you experience delayed or absent puberty, delayed development of sexual characteristics?

Treatment of Uterine Hypoplasia

Treatment varies based on symptoms. Uterine hypoplasia is typically caused by pubertal failure and may be treated with puberty induction using estrogens and/or progestogens.
