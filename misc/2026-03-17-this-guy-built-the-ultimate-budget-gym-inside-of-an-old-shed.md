# This Guy Built the Ultimate Budget Gym Inside of an Old Shed

Training at home as become more popular than ever during the pandemic, and a growing number of people are looking to set up their own at-home workout spaces in response to gym closures. In a recent YouTube video, Coop from the Garage Gym Reviews channel took viewers on a tour of the gym he created inside a utility shed in his back yard, and explained how he chose each piece of equipment to make the most efficient use of the limited space available.

[Budget Gym Inside](https://tendonforge.dns0663.com/blog/0846754926.html)

[This Guy](https://hostdefense-journal.escortskhi.com/)

The unit itself measures 16 x 12 feet. You could stand to go a bit smaller if you're short on space in your yard, but Coop recommends 10 x 10 as an absolute minimum, otherwise there won't be sufficient room to store and use your equipment. He also advises ensuring that it has weather-resistant walls, a metal roof, and ballistic tiles in the floor.

The first feature Coop points out is the gladiator rack, which he uses to store his chains, battle rope, a drag sled for outdoor workouts, and resistance bands. "I think for a budget setup, bands are one of your best, most versatile items," he says. "You can do so much to them; you can add them to a barbell, put them on the top rafters and do lat pulldowns, do different accessory movements, add a combination of resistance to my lifts."

[Inside of an Old](https://incretin-path.escortskhi.com/blog/4290361988.html)

In another corner of the shed, he has an assault bike for conditioning workouts—although this is not strictly essential, he adds: "I think for most people, if you're starting a budget gym, I'd go with a jump rope or something like that."

[Inside of an Old](https://nootropic-atlas.escortskhi.com/blog/2993208562.html)

Next to the bike are Coop's adjustable dumbbells, which he thinks are "perfect" when starting to equip a budget home workout space, and a kettle grip which he can then attach to his dumbbells to use as makeshift kettlebells.

Other equipment which he keeps stored in a shelving unit includes a plyo box for box jumps, a weighted vest, and a reverse hyper for posterior work. While the shed itself cost around $4,000, Coop estimates that it's possible to equip a home home gym for no more than $2,000.

[Built the Ultimate Budget](https://tissue-renewal.china-akan.com/)

Then comes the "most important part" of Coop's home gym, the squat rack, which doubles up as a pullup bar when he places a barbell at the top, and which includes a landmine attachment at the base. "That's what you should be thinking, bang for your buck in a small space," he says.
