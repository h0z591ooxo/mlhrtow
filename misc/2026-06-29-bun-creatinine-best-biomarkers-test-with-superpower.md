What is BUN/Creatinine Ratio?

The BUN/creatinine ratio is a calculated comparison between two waste products filtered by your kidneys: blood urea nitrogen (BUN) and creatinine. BUN comes from the breakdown of protein in your diet and tissues. Creatinine is a byproduct of muscle metabolism that's produced at a fairly steady rate.

Two waste products, one revealing pattern

Your kidneys clear both substances from your blood, but they handle them differently. When you compare their levels, the ratio reveals whether changes in kidney function are happening alone or alongside other factors like dehydration, bleeding, or protein intake.

Why is BUN/Creatinine Ratio important?

The BUN/creatinine ratio compares two waste products filtered by your kidneys, revealing whether kidney function itself is impaired or whether something upstream - like dehydration, heart failure, or bleeding - is stressing the system. A typical ratio sits between 10 and 20, with values in the middle range generally reflecting balanced kidney filtration and stable fluid status.

When the ratio drops below normal

A low ratio often signals dilution from overhydration, aggressive fluid therapy, or conditions like liver disease that reduce urea production. It can also appear in well-nourished individuals with high muscle mass who generate more creatinine relative to urea. Symptoms are usually subtle but may include swelling or confusion if fluid overload is severe.

Factors that influence interpretation

The ratio is sensitive to hydration status, recent protein intake, and acute illness. Medications like corticosteroids or diuretics can shift the balance. Older adults may show higher ratios due to reduced muscle mass and altered kidney blood flow. Context from symptoms and other lab values is essential for accurate interpretation.
