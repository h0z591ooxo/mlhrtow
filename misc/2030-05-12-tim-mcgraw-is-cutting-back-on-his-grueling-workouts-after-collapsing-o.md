Tim McGraw is going to dial back his intense workouts.

On Sunday, the country music legend collapsed onstage during a performance at the annual Country to Country festival in Dublin, Ireland. Twenty minutes later, his wife and fellow country great Faith Hill took to the stage to explain McGraw was suffering from dehydration and exhaustion: "We’ve all been a little bit dehydrated, traveling so much," she said. "He's been super dehydrated and I apologize, but I made the decision that he cannot come back out onstage."

When he returned to the U.S., McGraw told TMZ he was feeling "good, man." He blamed the collapse on "lots of flying and dehydration," adding: "I'll cut short my workouts."

McGraw may indeed be right about needing a rest day. After all, his workouts are truly something to behold.

Tim McGraw's Workouts

Back in 2015, McGraw told the New York Times that he travels everywhere with his portable gym (yes, just like The Rock).

McGraw said his workouts begin at 11 a.m. when he is on tour. He warms up by attaching "40-pound weights to his ankles and running up and down a hill."

He said that later in the day, he returns to his mobile gym where he works through a typical CrossFit routine including tractor tire flips, working the battle ropes, performing squats, burpees and deadlifts. If a pool is available, he added that he’ll hop in with his wife to perform something that resembles "CrossFit meets martial arts in the pool."

How to Prevent Dehydration

While McGraw's grueling CrossFit-style workouts sound tough, his hours-long performances could also have contributed to his collapse, according to Kristen Cavaco, a Certified Personal Trainer who holds degrees in Science Kinesiology, Exercise Science and a Doctorate of Physical Therapy, who doesn't treat the country music star.

"While it is not the traditional workout we think of, being on stage singing, dancing, under warm stage lights for hours causes a level of exertion of your body,” Cavaco told MensHealth.com.

"Combine his typical workout with poor hydration and add the second workout of the stage you have a recipe for passing out," she added.

According to Cavaco, any guy looking to prevent dehydration should follow a water routine that looks something like this: Two to three hours before working out, drink 16 ounces of water; 15 minutes before, drink 8 ounces; while working out, drink four ounces of fluid for every 15 to 20 minutes of exercise; and following your workout, drink down another 16 to 20 ounces of fluid. If that sounds like a lot of fluids, remember that Tom Brady reportedly drinks 25 glasses of water a day.

How do you know if you're dehydrated in the middle of a workout? A faster heart rate, dizziness, saggy skin, and smelly urine can all be signs that you need more fluids.
