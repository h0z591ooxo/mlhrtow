# Everything You Need to Know About Bucky Barnes, the Winter Soldier

Bucky Barnes, a.k.a. The Winter Soldier, remains one of Marvel's most interesting heroes

Actor Sebastian Stan told us his character even helps real-life veterans

Here’s everything else to know about the Winter Soldier before Disney +'s The Falcon and the Winter Soldier

Like most superheroes, James Buchanan "Bucky" Barnes was orphaned at a young age and acquired his powers after an accident nearly took his life. Unlike many superheroes, Barnes generally plays second fiddle to leading man Captain America, who’s both Barnes’ bestie and also someone he occasionally tries to murder with a knife when he's acting more as The Winter Soldier. Friendships are complicated sometimes.

Barnes is the Marvel character who’s been beat down, dusted, dismembered, and brain-hacked the most, making him something of an underdog, as well as a true super soldier. In fact, actor Sebastian Stan told us in a recent cover story that playing Barnes in the Marvel movies has been celebrated by combat veterans, who often tell Stan his character helps them cope with post-traumatic stress. Now that’s a real superpower.

Here’s everything else to know about him before the upcoming Disney+ series, The Falcon and the Winter Soldier, emerges from cryogenic sleep.

What are Bucky Barnes' powers?

As a soldier, Barnes' powers were his combat skills: hand-to-hand (he was a boxing champion), guns, grenades. During the war, Barnes is captured by HYDRA who performs experiments that eventually result in Barnes' non-human abilities.

After Steve Rogers (newly Captain America) rescues Barnes, the two friends set out to destroy HYDRA’s facilities. During one operation, Barnes and Rogers fight HYDRA agents on a moving train. Barnes is injured and falls hundreds of feet down into an icy river. Though Rogers assumes Barnes has perished, the experiments HYDRA conducted on him help him survive the fall.

How did Bucky become the Winter Soldier?

Barnes survives the fall, but his left arm is maimed—it was severed on impact. He is then discovered and returned to HYDRA for more experiments, this time also alongside S.H.I.E.L.D. This new experimental program, the Winter Soldier Program, outfits Barnes with a fancy new prosthetic arm and turns him into a mindless weapon. Barnes is frozen and then defrosted over and over again for the next few decades, carrying out assassination missions, including JFK and Iron Man’s daddy, Howard Stark.

After being sent to kill Nick Fury, a masked Barnes (still brainwashed) encounters both Black Widow and his old best bud, Captain America. The three battle it out. Then Rogers takes off Barnes’ mask and bam! He realizes he was fighting his old pal all along. Later, still fighting, Rogers tells Barnes what he said at his mother’s funeral. And bam! Barnes remembers who he is. Except the both of them plummet out of an aircraft into water moments later. Barnes saves Rogers and then disappears on a moody quest to discover who he is.

Is Bucky Barnes an Avenger?

Kind of? After he regains his memories, Barnes is blamed for an attack in Austria that kills King T'Chaka of Wakanda. Barnes then becomes something of a homicidal wild card, getting captured, getting brainwashed again, trying to kill everyone again, remembering, having everyone try to kill him; he basically causes the breakup of the Avengers, kind of.

After his arm is destroyed by Iron Man, Barnes is granted asylum in Wakanda where he goes back into his ice sleep to remove the rest of his Winter Soldier programming. Barnes emerges from his sleep cured, but is then recruited back into the fight as Thanos' forces march on Wakanda. At this point, we can finally consider Barnes an Avenger. And then he's dusted.

Does Bucky become Captain America?

After the dusting, Barnes returns for another battle with Thanos, where he and the other Avengers are finally successful. After the battle, Rogers returns to 1943, ages, and then reunites with Bucky. He hands his shield to Sam Wilson/Falcon as Bucky looks on proudly. And ... that's it. That's where we left him.
