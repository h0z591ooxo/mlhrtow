# No, Hans Wagner From ‘Army of Thieves’ Isn’t Real

Warning: This article contains spoilers for Netflix’s Army of Thieves.

Expanding the world of Army of the Dead, Army of Thieves is the second movie in Zack Snyder's zombie universe; this time, the story follows the ill-fated safe cracker Ludwig Dieter prior to the events of the first film. In the movie, Dieter wins his first safecracking tournament and is then approached by Gwendoline Starr, a jewelry thief who wants Dieter’s help in opening safes crafted by a renowned safe designer named Hans Wagner.

According to the film’s recounting, Wagner built four intricate Ring Cycle safes, each with increasing difficulty, starting with the Rheingold. He believed himself to be the illegitimate son of German composer Richard Wagner, and named each safe after Richard Wagner’s compositions. Toward the end of his life, Wagner created one final safe, then locked himself inside. His employees were unable to unlock the safe, and instead dropped it in the ocean to serve as Wagner’s tomb.

In Army of Thieves, Starr, Dieter, and their criminal associates manage to unlock three of the four Ring Cycle safes. Before the team can get to the fourth, Starr, Korina and Dieter are all found and captured by Interpol, but Starr convinces them to let Dieter free. That leaves the fourth and final safe ready for the taking in Army of the Dead.
