EVEN THOUGH HBO's Task comes from the same creator, Brad Inglesby, as Mare of Easttown, it was always going to be difficult to compare the two shows. While both are indeed set in the Delco/"Outside Philly" area, Mare's murder mystery structure is distinctly different from the slow burn cat-and-mouse thriller nature of Task. And the toughest part about any slow burn is all in the descriptor itself: It takes a while to get where it's going. But once it gets there, man oh man, it promises to be worth the wait.

That proves true in Task's sixth episode, "Out beyond ideas of wrongdoing and rightdoing, there is a river," which opens with the action-packed shootout/fight scene/set piece that the whole season has been building toward. We've got Robbie (Tom Pelphrey) making his last stand, we've got Tom (Mark Ruffalo) holding his ground with his titular team scattered, and we've got the villainous Dark Hearts in pursuit. There's a lot going on, and it's one of the most thrilling sequences you'll see on TV all year.

In a moment that's somewhat predictable—given that they're both technically protagonists of the show—we see Tom and Robbie team up, realizing that although they're on different sides of the law, in this moment (and maybe more broadly) they have a common enemy in the Dark Hearts. Each saves the other repeatedly, but they eventually get separated, taking on one primary antagonist each.

Does Robbie really die in Task?

Robbie does indeed die in the opening segment of Task episode 6. It's a bold move from Brad Inglesby to kill off one of his lead characters before the end of the show, but with so many different storylines and threads being juggled—including Tom's family situation, the in-fighting of the Dark Hearts, and Grasso's status as an informant and traitor—there's plenty that the show still needs to figure out. So catching the audience off guard and giving Robbie's storyline some real (if upsetting) closure and grace is a smart move.

"He has a very clear value system that he’s following no matter what he’s doing and however crazy it might be or however violent things might be, he’s only doing what he needs to do in his mind to provide for his children," Pelphrey told The Wrap in an interview. “He’s not interested in any unnecessary suffering. He doesn’t want anybody to be hurt or killed... it’s admirable the discipline of that and and he follows that through to the end, because that’s at the core of who he is."

He further elaborated in an interview with Decider. "I know I’m biased, but I love Robbie so much," he said. "I loved what he was trying to do so much and how he was trying to do it ."

Of course, the end of episode 6 reveals that Robbie may have always known his demise was inevitable. In the closing moments of the episode, Shelley (Mickey Sumner) brings Maeve (Emilia Jones) a duffel bag filled with cash, revealing that Robbie brought the Fentanyl to her in order to flip, and directed her to bring the money to Maeve; Perry and Jayson find Robbie's duffel bag filled with magazines.

"By the end of this, we love him so much and you feel like he has left some sort of legacy for his kids. That isn’t all bad,” Salli Richardson-Whitfield, who directed the episode, told Decider in the same piece. “Your eventual love for Robbie is earned, and that’s why it’s so heartbreaking."
