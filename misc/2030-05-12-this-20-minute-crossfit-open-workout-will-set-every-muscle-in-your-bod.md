# This 20-Minute CrossFit Open Workout Will Set Every Muscle In Your Body on Fire

Move over, Olympics: the CrossFit Open, which has been touted as one of the largest sporting events on Earth, has officially begun. During the five-week competition, 500,000 participants ranging from teens to 74-year-old masters test their strength and endurance for an opportunity to compete in the CrossFit Games later this year, allowing you to go head-to-head with the fittest athletes in the world.

[Minute CrossFit Open Workout](https://tiancansz.com/awyoylye/)

Every Thursday from Feb. 22 to Mar. 26, CrossFit super-villain Dave Castro releases a new workout, and athletes have until Monday to complete it and submit their scores to the global leaderboard. Last week, Castro kicked off with the first workout, 18.1, a 20-minute combination of as many reps as possible of toes-to-bar, dumbbell clean-and-jerks, and rowing.

I tried it for myself, and while I didn't finish feeling like my lungs were being ripped in half (perhaps a less than ideal start to the Open), I talked to Pat Vellner, the bronze medalist from the 2017 CrossFit Games, to figure out where I went wrong — and how you can learn from my mistakes and do it, too.

1) Keep the transitions tight, especially on and off the rower

Like many CrossFitters, I have a tendency to come out of the gates too hot. To keep myself from redlining and potentially burning out my grip, I put about eight feet of space between my bar, dumbbell, and rower. It was an irrational choice, but I wanted to be able to walk slowly between exercises and recover. Vellner advocates for the opposite approach: "Every time you finish a movement, go, transition fast, and try to stay really focused on what you're doing."

For quicker transitions on the rower, consider putting the straps in the "Goldilocks" zone: tight enough to hold your feet, but loose enough to slide in and out. And to get the wheel going, Vellner suggests doing two half-pulls — one with just arms and then one with just legs — and rowing normally.

2) Expect that you'll have to rest and know how and when to scale back

In order to stay moving, the most efficient strategy isn't necessarily the best, says Vellner. For instance, instead of moving from the clean directly into the jerk, consider pausing in between. "You can breathe with the dumbbell on your shoulder," he says. "And you can breath with it overhead. You can almost get two full breaths if you need to, so maybe do that one round to get your heart rate down, and then the next round you can go faster."

Vellner advises doing a few test rounds of the workout beforehand, then having back-up variations of each movement. He also suggests slowing down one movement, such as the clean-and-jerk, to hit the other one harder, "because you can't do all three movements all-out for twenty minutes." Again, it's better to be working slowly than not working at all.

3) Accept that not every workout needs to end in agony.

I've always thought that the Open was the perfect opportunity to push your fitness level. So when I was breathing normally five minutes after the workout, I felt like I'd failed and was planning on starting all over again.

But Vellner says that post-workout agony isn't always a good indicator of how hard you worked. This workout in particular demands a different kind of mental strength. "It was a challenge staying motivated because it felt like you were spinning in circles for the entire twenty minutes," he told me. "Every time you finished a round, you couldn't look at the clock because it'd only been ninety seconds. You had to keep your head down and keep chugging."

Maybe that's the lesson 18.1 was trying to teach me — how to keep chugging even when the workout isn't particularly sexy, even when there aren't flashy gymnastics movements or heavy Olympic lifts or a good opportunity to redline in the first 45 seconds. That's as much a part of CrossFit and the Open in particular as anything else: knowing when to charge forward, when to pull back, when to redo a workout, and when to accept the results and move on.
