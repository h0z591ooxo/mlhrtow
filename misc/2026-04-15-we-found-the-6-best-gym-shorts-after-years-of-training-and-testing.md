What to Consider

Material

Skip the cotton—it’s a sweat sponge. Synthetic fabrics like polyester blends are moisture-wicking and quick-drying, while nylon blends are more breathable for hot weather. Look for spandex or elastane for that four-way stretch, so you can move freely without restriction. Bonus: Some materials, like Merino wool, are naturally anti-stink, while others feature built-in odor-fighting technology to keep you smelling fresh, no matter how hard you go.

Liner or No Liner

Lined shorts offer built-in support, making them ideal for high-impact activities. "Unlined shorts offer more versatility for layering, while lined shorts provide additional support and comfort for high-impact activities," says Hamlin. If you’re lifting heavy or going ape on the air bike, the liner is a win. For casual workouts or layering over tights, unlined shorts let you move freely and stay cool.

Inseam

The inseam is the length of the shorts from your nether region to the inside hem, and getting it right can make or break your workout. If you’re doing moves like squats, lunges, or anything that’s got you moving deep and low, shorter inseams (think 5–7 inches) are your best bet for keeping things smooth and unrestricted. While shorter inseams allow for better mobility, longer inseams of 9–11 inches are great for taller athletes or anyone looking for extra coverage. Just keep in mind, if you’re on the shorter side, too much fabric can feel bulky and might even slow you down.

Functional storage is a must. "I say pockets are a must for me, and an extra bonus if they zip. Not a pocket in the back of my waist that I can barely reach, but pockets on the front sides," says Andy Stern from Rumble Boxing. Side pockets keep your essentials handy, and zippers ensure you’re not chasing keys mid-jog.

Fit and Feel

Your gym shorts should feel as good as they look. Elastic waistbands with drawstrings ensure a secure fit, while lightweight, breathable fabrics keep you comfortable and focused. Gym shorts should fit snugly around your waist—no squeezing, no sliding down mid-squat. Think "secure but free to move," like your gym buddy who knows when to spot you and when to back off. If you’re lunging and your shorts are riding up, it’s time to re-evaluate.

Activity-Specific Features

When it comes to picking gym shorts, it’s all about matching your moves. If you’re running, go for shorter inseams and split hems—you want freedom of movement, not fabric flapping around like you’re in a wind tunnel. Plus, reflective details keep you visible, because dodging traffic shouldn’t be part of your cardio. For lifting, you’ll need durable fabrics and compression liners to keep everything secure while you chase your personal best—nobody needs a wardrobe malfunction mid-squat. And for yoga? Think soft, stretchy, and unlined, so you can nail that downward dog without feeling like your shorts are plotting against you.

What Are the Most Flattering Shorts?

Flattering shorts come down to fit, proportion, and the right inseam for your build. If you’ve got beefy legs, short shorts will always work in your favor. Show them off a 5-inch inseam (or less), which hits mid-thigh for most guys. For a touch more coverage, go with a 7-inch inseam, the most popular length for a casual, everyday look. Aim for a slight taper through the leg to hold its shape without clinging. Bonus points for a smooth waistband that won’t dig in.
