Upper right abdominal pain in women is most commonly caused by gallbladder problems such as gallstones or cholecystitis (inflammation), but it can also stem from liver-related conditions like hepatitis or, in rarer cases, Fitz-Hugh-Curtis syndrome—a complication of pelvic inflammatory disease. Because these causes vary widely in urgency and treatment, identifying the source of your pain early is essential. Explanation

Several conditions can lead to pain in the upper right part of your belly. One common cause is problems with the gallbladder. Gallstones or inflammation of the gallbladder (cholecystitis) can make this area hurt. Ultrasound is often the first test used to look at these issues because it shows the gallbladder and nearby organs very well. Another possibility is liver-related pain. The liver is in the upper right part too, so liver problems can sometimes be confused with gallbladder pain. In women, a less common but notable cause is Fitz-Hugh-Curtis syndrome. This condition is linked with inflammation of the tissue that covers the liver and is sometimes seen with pelvic infections. It is important to remember that doctors look at your symptoms, medical history, and use imaging tests to sort out what exactly is causing the pain.
