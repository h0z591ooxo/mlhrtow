# SNL's 'Man Park' Sketch Makes a Good Point About Male Friendship

Like some of the best Saturday Night Live sketches, the recent "Man Park" has a simple, highly effective premise: the wives and girlfriends of straight men who have no guy friends take their partners to a dog park for humans so that they can play together.

In voiceover, commercial narrator Cecily Strong references real life studies which have found that many modern men struggle to maintain close friendships. "It's not their fault masculinity makes intimacy so hard,"she says. And while this kind of subject matter makes for a pretty decent joke, it is also coming from a very true place.

According to Cleo Stiller, author of Modern Manhood, this problem that men have with connecting and opening up to their friends as opposed to their romantic partners partially stems from the way that men process and articulate their emotions—or perhaps more accurately, the way that they don't. "Women are socialized to do this from a young age," she says. "Men not so much."

Sociologist Richard Schwartz agrees that emotional literacy has, for one reason or another, been feminized in culture. “On average, men are less comfortable expressing and sharing emotions than women are,” he told Men's Health. “They have less of a vocabulary for emotional things, and their friendships tend to be based on doing things together rather than sitting face-to-face and having a conversation about intimate things."

This is reflected in the sketch, in which the guys at the Man Park relate to each other by parroting stereotypically masculine aphorisms like "rise and grind", or simply stating their interests ("Marvel?" "Marvel.") rather than expressing their emotions.

Elliot J. Powell, the founder of Men2Health, believes that we need to jettison the old-fashioned ideas of masculinity which prize stoic silence over any kind of vulnerability. "As men, there’s a pressure not to show weakness, and a misunderstanding of what weakness is," he says. "Having emotions – and showing them – is not a weakness. Making mistakes or failing doesn’t always feel good, but you don’t need to deny those emotions. You just need to find the mindset to see it as growth. It’s not always positive things that get you to a good place."
