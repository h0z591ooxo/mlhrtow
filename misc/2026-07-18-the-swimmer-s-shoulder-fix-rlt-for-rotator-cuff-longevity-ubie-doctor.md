Swimmer's shoulder—often an overuse injury of the rotator cuff and surrounding structures—can sideline athletes of all levels. Understanding Swimmer's Shoulder

Swimmer's shoulder typically involves:

Rotator cuff tendinopathy – inflammation or microtears in the tendons.

Subacromial impingement – pinching of tendons under the shoulder blade.

Muscle imbalances – tightness in the front shoulder and chest, weakness in the back.

Practical RLT Protocols for Swimmers

To maximize benefits while staying safe, follow evidence-based dosing guidelines:

Wavelength: 800–850 nm for deeper penetration into rotator cuff tissues.

Power Density: 20–100 mW/cm².

Dose: 4–10 J/cm² per treatment site.

Session Duration: 5–15 minutes per shoulder, depending on device output.

Acute flare-ups: 4–5 sessions per week for 2–4 weeks.

Maintenance: 1–2 sessions per week once pain is under control.

Tip: Treat from multiple angles over the supraspinatus, infraspinatus, and subscapularis regions to ensure comprehensive coverage.

Safety and Considerations

Red light therapy is generally safe, but keep these points in mind:

Device quality: Use a certified medical-grade or FDA-cleared RLT device.

Eye protection: Wear goggles if treating near the face or neck.

Contraindications: Avoid over active cancer sites and unhealed fractures.

Skin monitoring: Rarely, mild redness or warmth may occur—reduce dose if needed.

Always follow the manufacturer's instructions. If you notice worsening pain, swelling, or unusual symptoms, discontinue use and seek professional advice.
