What Stomach Pains Should You Never Ignore?

Stomach pain is something almost everyone experiences at some point. Most of the time, it's mild, short-lived, and linked to everyday causes like indigestion, gas, stress, or a stomach bug. However, some types of stomach pain can signal a serious medical problem that needs prompt attention.

Understanding which stomach pains should never be ignored can help you act early, protect your health, and avoid complications. This guide is based on widely accepted medical guidance from credible clinical sources and is written to be clear, practical, and calm—without downplaying real risks.

When Stomach Pain Becomes Concerning

Not all stomach pain is equal. Doctors pay close attention to how the pain feels, where it is, how long it lasts, and what other symptoms are present. Pain that is severe, persistent, or unusual for you deserves closer attention.

In general, stomach pain should never be ignored if it:

Is sudden and severe

Gets worse instead of better

Comes with alarming symptoms

Interferes with normal daily activities

Feels different from pain you've had before

2. Stomach Pain With Fever

When stomach pain is paired with a fever, it often suggests infection or inflammation.

Diverticulitis

Gallbladder infection

Kidney infection

Severe food poisoning

You should be especially cautious if the fever is high, persistent, or accompanied by chills.

Action: Speak to a doctor, especially if the pain lasts more than 24 hours or worsens.

3. Stomach Pain With Persistent Vomiting or Nausea

Occasional nausea can happen with mild stomach pain, but ongoing vomiting is not normal.

Concerning signs include:

Inability to keep fluids down

Vomiting that lasts more than a day

Vomiting bile (green or yellow fluid)

Vomiting blood or material that looks like coffee grounds

These symptoms may indicate:

Bowel obstruction

Pancreatitis

Severe infection

Internal bleeding

5. Severe Pain in the Lower Right Abdomen

Pain in the lower right side of the abdomen is a classic sign of appendicitis, especially when it:

Starts near the belly button and moves right

Gets worse over several hours

Is accompanied by nausea or fever

Hurts more when you walk or cough

Appendicitis can become life-threatening if the appendix bursts.

Action: Do not ignore this pain. Seek urgent medical care.

6. Upper Right Stomach Pain After Eating

Stomach pain under the right ribs, especially after fatty meals, may point to gallbladder problems.

Concerning features:

Sharp or cramping pain

Pain that radiates to the back or shoulder

Nausea or vomiting

Yellowing of skin or eyes (jaundice)

Gallstones

Action: Speak to a doctor, especially if pain keeps returning or worsens.

7. Stomach Pain With Chest Pain or Shortness of Breath

Sometimes pain felt in the stomach is actually coming from the heart.

This is more likely if:

The pain is in the upper abdomen

You feel pressure or tightness

You have shortness of breath, sweating, or dizziness

You have risk factors like diabetes or heart disease

Action: This may be a medical emergency. Seek immediate care.

8. Stomach Pain That Wakes You at Night

Pain severe enough to wake you from sleep is more concerning than pain felt during the day.

Acid-related disorders

Certain infections

Action: Ongoing nighttime stomach pain should be evaluated by a healthcare professional.

9. Stomach Pain With Unintended Weight Loss

Losing weight without trying, especially along with stomach pain, should always be taken seriously.

Chronic infections

Digestive diseases

Hormonal disorders

Cancers of the digestive system

Action: Speak to a doctor for further evaluation.

When to Seek Immediate Help

You should seek emergency care for stomach pain if you experience:

Sudden, severe pain

Pain with fainting or confusion

Rigid or swollen abdomen

High fever with pain

Signs of internal bleeding

A Calm but Important Reminder

Most stomach pain is not dangerous, and many cases are treatable with simple care. However, ignoring serious stomach pain can delay diagnosis and make treatment more difficult.

If something feels severe, unusual, or frightening, trust your instincts. You are not overreacting by getting checked.
