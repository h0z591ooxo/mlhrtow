What is Nephrotic Syndrome?

A condition where the blood vessels in your kidneys get damaged and leak protein. Common causes include autoimmune (the body's immune system attacking itself) and diabetic kidney disease. It causes swelling in your body, usually in the feet and ankles.

Typical Symptoms of Nephrotic Syndrome

Previously diagnosed with Nephrotic Syndrome

Facial swelling

Having difficulty urinating or producing less urine

Taking steroids

Swelling of the arms or hands

Swollen eyelids

Diagnosed with Glomerulonephritis (IgA nephropathy or Nephrotic syndrome)

Diagnosed with Amyloidosis in the past

Diagnostic Questions for Nephrotic Syndrome

Your doctor may ask these questions to check for this disease:

Have you been diagnosed with Nephrotic Syndrome?

Is your face swollen?

Have you been experiencing difficulty urinating or producing less urine?

Are you currently taking steroids?

Are your hands or arms swollen?

Treatment of Nephrotic Syndrome

Treatment depends on the cause and extent of kidney damage. If it's caused by the immune system, the specialist may prescribe immune-suppressing medications. Medications to protect the kidneys and reduce water retention might also be given, such as blood pressure medications and diuretics (water pills).
