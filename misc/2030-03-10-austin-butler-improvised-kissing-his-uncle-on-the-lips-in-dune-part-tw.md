# Austin Butler Improvised Kissing His Uncle on the Lips in 'Dune: Part Two'

THERE IS NO shortage of weirdness in Frank Herbert's sci-fi epic Dune and Denis Villeneuve's ambitious screen adaptation. There's the psychedelic spice/melange which grants people the ability to see through time and space and navigate interstellar travel. The creepy order known as the Bene Gesserit who perform psychic eugenics in the hope of engineering the next messiah. The giant, not-at-all-phallic sandworms which slither beneath the deserts of Arrakis.

And then, while filming Dune: Part Two, actor Austin Butler decided to make things even weirder.

Butler plays Feyd-Rautha Harkonnen in the science fiction sequel, the younger brother of Rabban Harkonnen (Dave Bautista) and nephew of the villainous Baron Harkonnen (Stellan Skarsgård). In one of the film's stranger sequences—and that's saying a lot if you've seen it—the cruel and sadistic Feyd-Rautha usurps his brother and is granted rule of Arrakis by his uncle, the Baron. While in front of a cheering crowd, Feyd-Rautha then kisses his uncle on the lips.

[Kissing His Uncle on](https://matrix-skin.xianfengchem.com/)

It's a moment that wasn't included in the novel on which the film is based—although Herbert did write that the Harkonnens are known for their "full and pouting" lips—nor in the original script. Rather, Butler improvised the kiss on set—and as he later told reporters, Skarsgård was a receptive scene partner.

[Kissing His Uncle on](https://hostdefense-journal.escortskhi.com/)

"He's game for anything," Butler said. "He’s the best…it's always about how you're trying to affect somebody else."

Feyd-Rautha's ascent comes at the same time as his brother's fall from grace, and Dune: Part Two also shows the younger Harkonnen asserting his dominance by making Rabban kiss his feet. Just like Skarsgård, Dave Bautista was willing to do whatever the scene required.

"A lot of actors would be like, 'I don't want to do that,'" Bautista told USA Today. "But I thought it was so great. This is when I get to show who Rabban really is. He's just a coward. Right then, he's put in his place. You might have a moment where you actually feel sympathy for such a despicable character. So, yeah, I loved that scene."
