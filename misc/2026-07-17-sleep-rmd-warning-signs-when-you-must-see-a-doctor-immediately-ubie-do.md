Sleep-Related Rhythmic Movement Disorder (RMD) is a condition involving repetitive body movements—such as head banging, body rocking, or head rolling—that occur during sleep or as a person is falling asleep. While often harmless in young children, medical evaluation is recommended when RMD causes physical injury, significantly disrupts sleep quality, or interferes with daytime functioning like concentration, mood, or energy levels.

Explanation

Sleep-Related Rhythmic Movement Disorder (RMD) is a condition where people make repeated movements during sleep. These movements can include head banging, body rocking, or head rolling. It is most common in young children but can happen at any age.

Injury Risk: If the movements cause injury or have the potential to harm the person or others, it is important to see a doctor.

Sleep Disruption: If the movements are so frequent or intense that they disrupt sleep, it may be time for an evaluation.

Daytime Impact: If the disorder affects daytime activities, such as causing tiredness or affecting concentration, a doctor should be consulted.
