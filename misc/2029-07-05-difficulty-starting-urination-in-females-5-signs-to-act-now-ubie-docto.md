Difficulty starting urination in females, medically known as urinary hesitancy, is most commonly caused by non-neurogenic voiding dysfunction — a functional issue where the bladder and pelvic floor muscles don't coordinate properly. Age-related changes, especially in postmenopausal and older women, are another leading cause due to weakened pelvic muscles, hormonal shifts, and reduced bladder tone. Other contributing factors may include urinary tract infections, pelvic organ prolapse, or nerve-related conditions.

Because urinary hesitancy can stem from many overlapping causes, identifying the right one early makes a meaningful difference in treatment and comfort.
