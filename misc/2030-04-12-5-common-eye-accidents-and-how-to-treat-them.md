# 5 Common Eye Accidents, and How to Treat Them

Follow these sight-saving tips from James Tsai, M.D., Yale school of medicine, and Robert A. Goldberg, M.D., Jules Stein Eye Institute, UCLA.

SNAPPING BUNGEE You're rigging something to your roof rack. A hook slips loose and the cord, moving as fast as 166 mph, snaps into your eye. Damage: Bleeding in your eye, lens dislocation, traumatic cataract. The hook can penetrate and rupture the globe, risking blindness. Defense: Use a bungee with a carabiner instead of a hook, or nylon webbing with buckles, or buy some rope and learn a trucker's hitch knot.

CHEAP SHADES The football slips through your hands and hits your face. The lenses on your cheap sunglasses shatter. Damage: Lacerated cornea from the shards, and possible blunt ocular or orbital trauma from the ball. A baseball can fracture orbital bones. Defense: Lose the dollar-store Ray-Ban knockoffs and look for quality shatterproof lenses, like those available from Oakley or Smith Optics. (Click here for The Best Sport Sunglasses for Men.)

STRING TRIMMER While whacking weeds, you scrape the dirt, sending debris up into your eye. Damage: Lacerated cornea from small rocks. Other debris can scratch the cornea, leaving your eye vulnerable to infection. Defense: Treat string trimmers like the power tools they are, and use proper safety goggles. Sunglasses don't cut it; debris can enter from the sides.

FISHHOOK Your snagged hook snaps back into your eye. Fishing accounts for one in five sports-related eye injuries (watch lures and weights too). Damage: Corneal laceration, globe rupture, bleeding, cataract. It can lead to retinal detachment or possible long-term vision loss. Defense: Never yank a taut line. Hook in the eye? Tape the hook to your brow, cover the eye with a cup. Cover the good eye so your eyes stay still.

PAINTBALL Your goofball buddy declares an impromptu war, and you engage him without goggles. You take a shot to the eye. Damage: Bleeding; blunt trauma, lens dislocation, cataract, globe rupture, possible blindness (including risk of retinal detachment). Defense: Never load a gun without donning full-coverage goggles that protect the top, bottom, and sides—like those at commercial courses.
