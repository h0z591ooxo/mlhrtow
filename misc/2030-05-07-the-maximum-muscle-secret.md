# The Maximum Muscle Secret

[Maximum Muscle](https://brainmessenger.xianfengchem.com/)

To pack on more muscle, slow down—way down—with an exercise called the tempo squat. It’ll target your slow-twitch muscle fibers in a way that builds a surprising amount of size.

How so? First, a quick explanation: Your body has two main types of muscle fibers. Fast-twitch fibers are used during powerful movements like jumping and heavy resistance training. They have a greater capacity for growth than slow-twitch fibers, which are used in endurance sports like distance running.

[Maximum Muscle](https://cortexcompass.jcesqc8.com/blog/5751652268.html)

Because of that, many experts say to target fast-twitch muscle fibers if you want to pack on pounds. (And intuitively, most guys realize they need to lift heavy to get big.)

But another method is to also target your slow-twitch muscle fibers. “While slow-twitch fibers don’t grow as much as fast-twitch, they still contribute to muscle size,” says Bill Hartman, P.T., C.S.C.S., the co-owner of Indianapolis Fitness and Sports Training and creator of the Russian Fat-Loss Secret Workout. And that’s what many guys don’t realize.

[Muscle Secret](https://mimi567.com/44dvmwq/)

This is where the tempo squat comes in. By moving slowly up and down—without pausing at the top of bottom of the exercise—you keep your muscles in a constant state of tension for an extended period. Do this often enough, and it will increase the size of your slow-twitch fibers. It also causes a rise in lactate levels during the movement. Lactate is your body’s buffering agent for the acid that builds up in your legs during the squat and makes them burn. (This was commonly thought of as lactic acid, but scientists no longer believe that’s true.) Higher lactate levels can actually be a good thing. “As much as 80 percent of your body’s lactate is processed and becomes an energy source in the slow-twitch fibers,” Hartman says. Build bigger slow-twitch fibers, and your body is better able to use lactate for fuel. The payoff: You can go harder for longer.

Tip: Start with about 50 percent of your one-rep max loaded onto the bar. That’s plenty to make your legs feel like they’re on fire.
