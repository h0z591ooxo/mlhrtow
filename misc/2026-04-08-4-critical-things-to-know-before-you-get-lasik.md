# 4 Critical Things to Know Before You Get LASIK

When Americans get laser eye surgery to correct their vision, most go with LASIK, since it’s a household name and promises fast recovery. The trouble: Dry eyes are significantly more prevalent with LASIK than with other surgeries, and I’ve only seen that increase with more screen time. PRK and ASA (advanced surface ablation) shape the cornea without a cut, resulting in far less damage and dryness. That’s not to say that LASIK is wrong for everyone, but it’s important to do your research—homework is the “secret” to every A+ laser vision correction outcome. Use these strategies before you sign up for any laser vision-correction surgery.

Consider your eyes, lifestyle, and career

The right vision-correction surgery must reflect the patient’s individual eyes and lifestyle. Ask about other procedures before deciding on one. The doctor needs to consider the thickness of your cornea and your type of vision trouble. For instance, if you have a job where clear vision—especially at night—is important (for instance, you’re in aviation or transportation, or you’re a first responder), then the halos, haze, and starbursts that can appear after LASIK spell trouble. If you play contact sports—or have a baby—an accidental poke to the eye can dislodge the corneal flap left by LASIK. I also think that people who spend most of their days working on the computer aren’t good candidates for LASIK. When you combine the propensity for dry eyes from that procedure with eight to 14 hours of screen time per day, the result could be chronically dry eyes. If your doctor isn’t willing to recommend alternatives, find a new doc.

Check their experience

Ask the doctor how many times they’ve corrected prescriptions similar to yours. You never want to have the highest prescription that a surgeon has ever operated on. Your surgeon should recognize the limitations of laser eye surgery for very high prescriptions.

Make sure there’s follow-up

Some surgery centers tend to rely on surgeons who perform your operation and then you don’t come in contact with them again; you’re left to communicate with trainees. Like any procedure, there are possible issues that can arise during your recovery period that are best addressed by the surgeon.

Find out how long recovery takes

Make sure your doc is clear that PRK and ASA require a longer recovery period because the corneal surface needs to heal. LASIK’s recovery period is almost immediate, but you live with the effects of the flap cut. Base your decision on your vision and well-being three months or three years post-op instead of three hours or three days. And don’t be swayed by big discounts. Researching and discussing a procedure in detail gives you a much higher chance that your laser eye surgery will dramatically improve your life.
